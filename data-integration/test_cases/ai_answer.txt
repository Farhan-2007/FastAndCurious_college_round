The Rivertown bike-share pilot ran for six months, starting in March 2025. It deployed 400 bicycles across 25 docking stations. The pilot cost INR 4.2 crore. More than twelve thousand people signed up as registered users. Riders made 186,000 trips in total. The average trip lasted 25 minutes. Central Station was the busiest location, accounting for 14% of all trips. Most trips took place on weekdays. With 186,000 trips and 400 bicycles, each bicycle averaged roughly 465 trips over the pilot. The pilot attracted 12,500 users, 62% of whom were commuters. Cycling is good for cardiovascular health.
The program was most popular among university students. 37 bicycles were stolen. Only 12 bicycles were damaged. Maintenance crews responded within one hour on average. Since 78% of surveyed users were satisfied, the pilot significantly reduced traffic congestion. Many users asked for night-time availability. The report did not measure the effect on air quality. The city council has already approved expanding to 40 stations. Because damaged bicycles were rare, riders can safely skip pre-ride inspections. The report was prepared in September 2025.
Harbour Road was the second busiest station, with 18,600 trips. The five busiest stations handled 48% of all trips. Fare revenue covered about 13% of the pilot's cost. Helmets were supplied with every bicycle. No serious injuries were reported during the pilot. The pilot was run entirely by the municipal office without any contractor. The most common complaint was bicycle availability during peak hours. The findings show that a full year of operation would give similar results. Fare revenue was low.
